Phys. Rev. A 97, 043401 (2018) & Honors Thesis (2019)

Expansion of an Ultracold Rydberg Plasma

G. T. Forest, Y. Li, E. D. Ward, A. L. Goodsell, D. A. Tate — and the author’s own 2019 Colby College honors thesis of the same title

Code on GitHub → Read the paper →

The physics

Laser-cool a cloud of atoms, then photoexcite a fraction of them to high-lying Rydberg states, and the cloud becomes an ultracold neutral plasma: a mix of ions, free electrons, and Rydberg atoms, all far colder than any plasma occurring naturally. That coldness is exactly what makes it interesting — the electron gas starts out with a Coulomb coupling parameter Γe (the ratio of Coulomb interaction energy to thermal energy) that can approach or exceed 1, putting the system at the boundary between a weakly-coupled classical plasma and a strongly-coupled one, a regime hard to reach any other way.

What happens next is a competition between several processes happening on comparable timescales. Electrons recombine with ions in three-body collisions, releasing binding energy that reheats the electron gas (disorder-induced heating counteracts this cooling too). The newly formed Rydberg atoms are struck again by free electrons, scattering them among different (n,l) states and further exchanging energy with the electron bath. Those Rydberg atoms also radiatively decay through the full hydrogenic cascade. All of this happens while the plasma itself expands outward under its own electron thermal pressure. The central question — both in the 2018 paper and in the author’s 2019 honors thesis extending it — is how the initial electron temperature and the Rydberg binding energy set that coupling strength, and therefore the plasma’s subsequent heating and hydrodynamic expansion.

The simulation

This is a from-scratch Monte Carlo kinetic model (a variant of the Robicheaux model used across this subfield), written in C++ rather than Python. It has no figures of its own — it writes raw time-series data files — so here is the pipeline each timestep runs:

1. Three-body recombination (Mueller–Wolf rates) — electron–ion–electron collisions form new Rydberg atoms and heat the electron gas with the released binding energy.

2. Electron–Rydberg scattering / l-mixing (Mansbach–Keck rates) — free electrons collide with existing Rydberg atoms, redistributing population across the (n,l) manifold and exchanging further energy with the electron bath.

3. Radiative cascade decay — each Rydberg atom decays through the full hydrogenic (n,l) ladder using tabulated decay rates (data/radratesH.dat, ~9,000 transition rates) sourced from hydrogenic state energies (data/energiesH.dat).

4. Hydrodynamic expansion — the ion cloud, modeled as an expanding Gaussian, is advanced one step via 2nd-order Runge–Kutta integration, driven by the electron thermal pressure computed from the updated Coulomb coupling parameter.

All physical run parameters — initial temperature, cloud size, density, run duration, initial Rydberg fraction — are edited directly as named constants at the top of the source file rather than passed as command-line arguments, a design inherited unchanged from the original code.
